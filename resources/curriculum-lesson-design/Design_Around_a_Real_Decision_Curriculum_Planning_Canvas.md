# Design Around a Real Decision
## Curriculum Planning Canvas

A reusable backward-design tool for courses, workshops, lessons, and learning resources. Start with what the learner needs to understand or do in a real context, then work backward to the evidence, skills, practice, and support required.

Use this at three levels:

- **Program / curriculum:** What larger decisions, performances, or transitions should the full sequence prepare learners for?
- **Unit / module:** What meaningful intermediate capability does this part of the sequence build?
- **Lesson / session:** What can learners practice, produce, decide, or demonstrate now that moves them toward transfer?

## 0. Learner context & starting conditions

Before designing content, identify what is true about the learners and learning environment.

**Who are the learners?**

**What do they already know or do?**

**What do they need, want, or expect to be able to do?**

**What relevant differences may exist in literacy, language, prior education, digital access, professional experience, confidence, disability/access needs, or familiarity with the context?**

**What information can be gathered before the learning experience?**

**What must be diagnosed during the first session/activity instead?**

**What information is private, sensitive, or unnecessary to collect?**

**Available time / modality / technology / staffing / materials:**

### Quality check

- [ ] Learner assumptions are based on evidence rather than stereotypes.
- [ ] The design does not require unnecessary personal information.
- [ ] Required technology and materials are realistically available.
- [ ] Support is planned for likely prerequisite gaps.
- [ ] The design can adapt when a cohort differs from expectations.

## 9. Content filter

For every proposed reading, video, lecture, activity, example, or resource, ask:

**Resource/activity:**

It helps the learner:

- [ ] Understand
- [ ] Find evidence
- [ ] Practice
- [ ] Decide
- [ ] Create / perform
- [ ] Receive/use feedback
- [ ] Transfer

If none applies, why is it here?

Can it be removed, shortened, or moved to optional reference material?

## 11. Access & inclusion

- [ ] Instructions are understandable without hidden background knowledge.
- [ ] Important information is not communicated through color alone.
- [ ] Documents/materials have usable heading structure and readable contrast.
- [ ] Media has appropriate captions/transcripts/alternatives.
- [ ] Learners have more than one reasonable way to engage where the objective permits it.
- [ ] Time pressure is used only when speed is part of the actual competency.
- [ ] Examples do not assume one cultural, educational, professional, or economic background.
- [ ] Required technology is necessary, available, and supported.
- [ ] Accessibility supports do not accidentally change the competency being assessed.

## Development note

This 2026 resource is a newly authored derivative informed by recurring design patterns documented in Laura Elizabeth Hand’s archived curriculum work. Surviving Community Impact materials pair practical tasks and decisions with language objectives, learner artifacts, feedback, and subsequent application; class-summary records also capture topic, resources, learner reaction/mastery, additional exercises, and comments for the next instructor. Later ELL materials explicitly incorporate learner needs, digital-literacy conditions, scaffolding, instructor notes, assessment/compliance requirements, and adaptation across synchronous/asynchronous contexts.

This canvas generalizes those methods for broader instructional-design use. It is not a reproduction of an institutional handout, textbook, or proprietary curriculum.
