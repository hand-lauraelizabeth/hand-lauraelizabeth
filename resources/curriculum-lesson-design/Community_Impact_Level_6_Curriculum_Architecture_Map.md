# Community Impact Level 6 Curriculum Architecture Map

A curriculum architecture map of the **40-lesson adult ESOL sequence** used at Community Impact in Summer 2019.

The map focuses on sequence and instructional architecture: how practical adult-learning goals, language objectives, learner work, and instructor handoff fit together.

## Curriculum progression

| Chapter | Lessons | Practical focus | Language / learning pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1–4 | Orientation, introductions, listening, goal-setting | Classroom language, listening, self-assessment, goal language |
| 2 | 5–8 | Career sectors, career families, technology and labor markets | Comparison, prediction, sector vocabulary, evidence-based discussion |
| 3 | 9–12 | Career research, traits, employer types | Research organization, paraphrase/direct speech, compare/contrast |
| 4 | 13–16 | Career maps, résumé and cover-letter development | Past/present-perfect contrast, action verbs, employment writing |
| 5 | 17–20 | Informational interviewing, narratives, goal check-in, job ads | Question formation, indirect questions, conditionals, career reading |
| 6 | 21–24 | Mock interviews, job offers, peer review, workplace expectations | Hypotheticals, hedging, peer feedback, obligation/modals |
| 7 | 25–28 | Workplace communication, entrepreneurship, generations | Conditional forms, discussion/disagreement, gerunds/infinitives |
| 8 | 29–32 | Workers’ rights, child development, paid family leave, debate | Transitions/connectors, evidence, concession, structured argument |
| 9 | 33–36 | Family literacy, child language, intergenerational education | Descriptive language, reading strategies, textual evidence |
| 10 | 37–40 | High-school equivalency, CUNY pathways, program requirements, next steps | Institutional vocabulary, comparison, research, planning |

## Repeating design pattern

Across the sequence, the curriculum repeatedly uses the following structure:

**Authentic adult decision or task**  
→ **language/rhetorical capability**  
→ **guided activity or organizer**  
→ **learner-produced artifact or performance**  
→ **feedback / peer review / discussion**  
→ **later reuse in a more complex context**

### Examples

| Authentic task | Language / reasoning | Learner work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare career sectors | Comparison structures + labor vocabulary | Sector comparison and written response |
| Research a career | Source use + paraphrase/direct speech | Research organizer |
| Build a career map | Time/experience language | Career-map artifact |
| Write a résumé | Action verbs + employment vocabulary | Revised résumé content |
| Conduct informational interviews | Question formation | Interview-question set |
| Evaluate a job offer | Hedging + conditions + criteria | Written decision/response |
| Understand workplace expectations | Modals of obligation | Scenario analysis |
| Discuss workers' rights | Transitions/connectors + evidence | Evidence-based writing |
| Debate a policy issue | Concession + argument structure | Structured debate |
| Research education pathways | Comparison + institutional research | Next-step plan |

## Instructor implementation loop

Class-summary records show an operational feedback structure:

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Date / teacher | Establishes delivery context |
| Theme / topic | Locates the lesson in the sequence |
| Grammar / vocabulary / resources | Records the instructional approach |
| Alternatives | Preserves adaptation options |
| Class reaction / mastery | Captures learner-response evidence |
| Additional exercises | Records needed reinforcement |
| Comments for next teacher | Makes handoff explicit |

This turns curriculum delivery into an iterative process rather than a one-time lesson file.

## Design implications

The architecture suggests five reusable principles:

1. **Context before abstraction.** Use real adult decisions to create a reason for language practice.
2. **Sequence across lessons.** Early research and goal-setting should feed later artifacts and decisions.
3. **Pair content and communication.** A practical topic and a language/rhetorical skill can be designed together.
4. **Support the instructor system.** Curriculum quality depends on onboarding, adaptation guidance, and handoff.
5. **Treat delivery evidence as design input.** Mastery, learner reaction, and instructor notes should inform revision.
