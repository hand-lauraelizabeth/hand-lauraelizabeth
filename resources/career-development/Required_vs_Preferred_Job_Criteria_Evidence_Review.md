# Required vs. Preferred: Job Criteria Evidence Review

*A practical, inspectable worksheet for interpreting job advertisements without manufacturing qualifications.*

**Purpose.** A job posting may mix minimum qualifications, preferences, responsibilities, and marketing language in the same paragraph. This worksheet separates what the employer explicitly requires from what they would like, then asks what the candidate's real evidence actually supports. It is not an applicant tracking system or a prediction of a hiring decision.

## 1. Establish the requirement boundary

Copy a posting with its original headings and punctuation. Review *each clause*, not just whole lines. Keep section headings attached to the bullets below them until another heading begins.

| Classification | How to recognize it | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Required** | The posting explicitly says required, minimum, must, mandatory, essential, or places an unambiguous qualification under a **Required Qualifications** heading | That possession is confirmed by a résumé keyword |
| **Preferred** | It says preferred, desired, nice to have, a plus, or appears under a **Preferred Qualifications** heading | A hard disqualification just because evidence is absent |
| **Responsibility or scope** | Describes work the person would perform, manage, create, or own | That the candidate has previously held the same title or decision rights |
| **Tool or method** | Specifies an application, technology, practice, or analytical technique | Advanced proficiency solely from a single mention |
| **Ambiguous / mixed** | A clause blends required and preferred terms or lacks clear scoping | A mandatory gate without direct textual evidence |

**Segmentation example** — *Bachelor’s degree required; master’s preferred.*

- **Required:** Bachelor's degree — confirm credential type, institution, and evidence.
- **Preferred:** Master's degree — document if relevant; do not label its absence “failed hard requirement.”
- If a posting says “Master's preferred **or equivalent experience**,” preserve both the preference and the alternative rather than splitting away its context.
- If the posting states “Master's degree required,” treat it as a required credential.
- If a section says **Preferred Qualifications** followed by bullet points, those bullets remain preferred until a new heading or explicit instruction changes that classification.

## 2. Apply the evidence review

Copy and extend this table for each opportunity. Use **Not visible**, not **Not qualified**, unless a requirement truly cannot be met after checking reliable evidence.

| Exact posting clause | Class | Evidence source and page/section | Visibility / verification | Real gap or visibility gap? | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bachelor's degree required | Required | Degree entry and registrar record | Visible; corroboration needed | Verification | Check degree entry/records |
| Master's preferred | Preferred | Candidate evidence not yet supplied | Not visible | Optional evidence gap, **not** hard-condition failure | Check transcripts/CV only if relevant |
| 8 years of experience required | Required | Roles listed 2017–2026 | Date ranges reviewed, elapsed time unresolved | Duration verification | Resolve exact months, relevant work, gaps and overlapping roles |
| Lead a cross-functional team | Scope | Project report | Claimed; authority unclear | Scope documentation | Identify direct reports versus coordination, decisions, and artifacts |

## 3. Review claim strength before using wording

1. **Chronology:** Count elapsed relevant experience from actual dates, not the number of calendar years touched. Overlapping roles do not double the elapsed total. A range like 2017–2026 is insufficient by itself for an exact-duration claim.
2. **Authority:** Distinguish managing a person, leading a project, and influencing an executive decision. Do not convert “supported” into “owned.”
3. **Quantification:** Recheck the numerator, denominator, time period, attribution, and source. Label estimates clearly.
4. **Source quality:** Separate self-attested history, direct artifacts, independently corroborated records, and reviewed public information. Do not describe unverified facts as verified.
5. **Confidentiality:** Do not paste private student, applicant, client, employer, or personnel information into public files. Use synthetic examples or sanitized descriptions.
6. **Accessibility and parsing:** Verify logical heading order and selectable text when preparing the actual résumé; no generic ATS score proves how a particular employer's system will process the file.

## 4. Make a defensible application decision

Before submitting, write down:
- **Mandatory conditions:** explicitly required, met with evidence, unknown/needs confirmation, or genuinely unmet.
- **Preferred evidence:** strengths to surface, optional gaps, and what is worth building later.
- **Scope matches:** specific projects with verified authority, outcomes, and dates.
- **Application questions:** work authorization, location, degree/licensing requirements, and salary/availability, verified against the actual employer form.

A posting alone does not disclose an employer's screening configuration. This worksheet supports judgment and clarity, not automated eligibility or hiring guarantees.
