# Research Project Roadmap

A start-to-finish routing guide for research projects. Use it to decide **what to do next**, which planning tool you need, and when a project is ready to move forward.

This is a newly authored public resource informed by research-writing instruction and project-planning practice. It is designed to work across humanities, social-science, applied, and mixed-methods projects. Always follow the requirements of your course, institution, journal, funder, or review body when they differ.

## Quick route

| If you are trying to... | Start with... |
| --- | --- |
| Understand the assignment, deliverable, or deadline | Stage 0: Define the project |
| Turn a topic into a workable question | Stage 1: Frame the question |
| Find and organize scholarly evidence | Stage 2: Map the evidence |

## Stage 0 — Define the project before researching it

Write down:

- **Deliverable:** paper, proposal, report, presentation, poster, thesis section, article, etc.
- **Audience:** instructor, disciplinary readers, client, public audience, review committee, etc.
- **Required length / format:** pages, words, citation style, sections, visuals, appendices.
- **Deadline:** final due date plus any proposal, draft, ethics, or peer-review checkpoints.
- **Evaluation criteria:** what will count as a strong result?
- **Constraints:** approved sources, data access, participant access, software, cost, time, collaboration, confidentiality.

### Gate 0

Do not begin broad searching until you can state the deliverable, audience, deadline, and main constraints.

## Stage 1 — Frame the research question

Start broad, then narrow.

**Topic:**  
**Problem / tension:**  
**What is already known or assumed?**  
**What remains unclear, contested, missing, or worth testing?**  
**Research question:**  
**Why does answering it matter?**

Test the question:

- Is it answerable with evidence you can actually obtain?
- Is it narrow enough for the available time and length?
- Does it require description, interpretation, comparison, explanation, prediction, evaluation, or intervention?
- Does the wording quietly assume the answer?
- What evidence would make you revise or reject your initial view?

### Gate 1

Move forward when the question is specific enough to guide source selection, but still open enough that the research can change your answer.

## Stage 2 — Map the evidence landscape

Separate **finding sources** from **using evidence**.

Track:

1. **Source** — what is it?
2. **Context** — who produced it, when, where, and for what purpose?
3. **Method / basis** — how was the claim produced?
4. **Relevant finding or passage** — what does it actually show?
5. **Use** — background, method, example, support, complication, contradiction, counterargument?
6. **Limits** — what can this source not establish?
7. **Traceability** — where can you return to verify the claim?

For a substantial literature review, use the [Literature Review & Evidence Synthesis Matrix](../literature-review/Literature_Review_Evidence_Synthesis_Matrix.xlsx).

### Gate 2

Do not treat a stack of summaries as a literature review. Move forward when you can identify patterns, disagreements, methodological differences, gaps, or relationships across sources.

## Stage 3 — Methods, ethics & feasibility

Ask what kind of evidence your question requires.

### If the project uses existing texts, archives, public records, or published datasets

Document:

- corpus / source-selection rule;
- inclusion and exclusion criteria;
- provenance and citation;
- missing or inaccessible material;
- interpretive or measurement decisions;
- reproducibility requirements.

### If the project involves people, private/identifiable data, recruitment, observation, interviews, surveys, experiments, or sensitive information

Use the [Research Project Ethics & IRB Readiness Toolkit](../research-ethics-irb/Research_Project_Ethics_and_IRB_Readiness_Toolkit.xlsx) **before** collecting data.

The toolkit is a planning aid, not an IRB determination. Institutional review requirements control.

### If the project includes quantitative analysis

Use the [Quantitative Research & Statistical Analysis Planner](../quantitative-analysis/Quantitative_Research_Statistical_Analysis_Planner.xlsx) to define variables, hypotheses, data-QA rules, candidate analyses, assumptions, effect-size reporting, and an SPSS-ready variable dictionary.

### Gate 3

Do not collect first and decide on ethics, variables, exclusions, or analysis later when those decisions can reasonably be made in advance.

## Stage 4 — Build the evidence record

For every important claim, preserve enough information to audit it later.

**Claim / question supported:**  
**Source or dataset:**  
**Exact evidence / variable / passage:**  
**Location or identifier:**  
**Interpretation:**  
**Alternative interpretation:**  
**Limitation:**  
**Citation / provenance complete?** Yes / No

Use the [Research Writing Planner & Evidence Matrix](Research_Writing_Planner_and_Evidence_Matrix.xlsx) for project-level tracking.

### Gate 4

You should be able to answer: **Where did this claim come from, and what would I inspect to verify it?**

## Stage 5 — Analyze before drafting conclusions

Choose the analysis that matches the question and evidence.

### Qualitative / textual

Look for:

- patterns and exceptions;
- repeated concepts or structures;
- contradictions;
- chronology and change;
- relationships among form, context, and meaning;
- competing interpretations;
- cases that weaken your preferred reading.

### Quantitative

Predefine, when possible:

- outcome and predictor variables;
- groups / timepoints / pairing;
- exclusions and missing-data treatment;
- descriptive statistics;
- primary vs. exploratory analyses;
- assumptions and diagnostics;
- uncertainty and effect sizes;
- multiple-testing or model-selection issues.

### Mixed methods

State what each evidence type contributes and how the parts will be integrated. Do not use one method merely to decorate conclusions reached by another.

### Gate 5

Move to conclusions only when the analysis can produce an answer that is more specific than the original topic.

## Stage 6 — Build the argument architecture

Before writing polished prose, map:

**Main claim / answer:**  
**Reason 1:**  
**Evidence:**  
**What the evidence permits you to claim:**  
**Limitation / qualification:**  
**Reason 2:**  
**Evidence:**  
**Counterargument / alternative explanation:**  
**Response:**  
**Implication:**  

For an APA student paper, start drafting in the [APA 7 Student Research Paper Template](APA7_Student_Research_Paper_Template.docx).

### Evidence test

For each paragraph or section, ask:

- What is this section trying to establish?
- What evidence does it use?
- Does the evidence actually support that claim?
- Is the reasoning visible, or does the reader have to supply it?
- What qualification would make the claim more accurate?

## Stage 7 — Draft, revise, and verify

Treat drafting and revision as different tasks.

### Drafting pass

Focus on:

- argument order;
- evidence placement;
- explanation;
- transitions between ideas;
- missing sections.

### Revision pass

Focus on:

- claim accuracy;
- structure;
- synthesis;
- paragraph purpose;
- counterevidence;
- concision;
- source integration.

Use the [Stronger Writing: Revision & Proofreading Checklist](../revision-proofreading/Stronger_Writing_Revision_and_Proofreading_Checklist.md).

### Verification pass

Check every:

- quotation;
- number;
- table / figure;
- source attribution;
- citation;
- proper noun;
- date;
- statistical result;
- link;
- claim of causation, significance, novelty, or consensus.

## Stage 8 — Submission QA

Before submitting:

- [ ] The deliverable matches the assignment / venue requirements.
- [ ] The research question and final answer still match.
- [ ] Every major claim has traceable support.
- [ ] Evidence is distinguished from interpretation.
- [ ] Limitations are stated where they matter.
- [ ] Human-participant / privacy requirements were resolved when relevant.
- [ ] Tables, figures, appendices, and references are present and labeled.
- [ ] Citations have been checked against the source.
- [ ] File name, format, title page, pagination, and submission method are correct.
- [ ] A final copy and supporting evidence record are preserved.

## Minimum viable research plan

When time is limited, complete these ten fields before doing anything else:

1. Deliverable
2. Audience
3. Deadline
4. Research question
5. Evidence needed
6. Source-selection rule
7. Method / analysis approach
8. Ethics / privacy check
9. Five major milestones
10. Definition of done
