Rubric Calibration Lab

Compare two independent ratings of the same fictional student response. See where the ratings diverge, then write a clearer criterion. This is a teaching exercise, not an automated judgment of student work.

Fictional sample

A learner argues that a workshop improved confidence. They cite a post-session survey of 18 respondents but do not report a baseline, comparison group, or response rate. They conclude that the workshop caused the improvement.

No student data is collected or transmitted. The example is invented.

Criterion: Use of evidence

4 — Claim is proportionate to evidence and limitations are explicit.
3 — Relevant evidence supports the claim; some limitations are noted.
2 — Some relevant evidence, but the conclusion exceeds what it establishes.
1 — Claim is unsupported or evidence is unrelated.

Feedback

Choose both ratings to compare them.

Applied capstone

Use the disagreement to revise one rubric descriptor, then test it on a second response. A useful descriptor names observable evidence rather than relying on adjectives such as “strong” or “adequate.”

Self-check: Does the revised descriptor distinguish observation from inference, explain how to handle missing information, and permit two reviewers to reach the same rating?
