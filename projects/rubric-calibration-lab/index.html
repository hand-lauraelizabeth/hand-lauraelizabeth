Rubric Calibration Lab

Compare two independent ratings of the same fictional student response. See where the ratings diverge, then write a clearer criterion. This is a teaching exercise, not an automated judgment of student work.

Fictional sample

A learner argues that a workshop improved confidence. They cite a post-session survey of 18 respondents but do not report a baseline, comparison group, or response rate. They conclude that the workshop caused the improvement.

No student data is collected or transmitted. The example is invented.

Criterion: Use of evidence

4 — Claim is proportionate to evidence and limitations are explicit.
3 — Relevant evidence supports the claim; some limitations are noted.
2 — Some relevant evidence, but the conclusion exceeds what it establishes.
1 — Claim is unsupported or evidence is unrelated.

Calibration decision

After comparing, record a shared score and the evidence that justifies it.

Feedback

Choose both ratings to compare them.

Calibration question: Which difference should change the rating, and which missing evidence matters in both cases?

Applied capstone: transfer your criterion

Test your revised descriptor on a different fictional response. A useful descriptor names observable evidence rather than relying on adjectives such as “strong” or “adequate.”

Second fictional response: “After a four-week course, 12 of 15 respondents said they felt more prepared to interview. Because only 15 of 24 participants responded, and no pre-course measure was collected, this describes respondents’ perceptions rather than demonstrating an improvement caused by the course.”

Choose a rating, then compare it with the first example.

Self-check: Does your revised descriptor distinguish observation from inference, explain how to handle missing information, and permit two reviewers to reach the same rating?
