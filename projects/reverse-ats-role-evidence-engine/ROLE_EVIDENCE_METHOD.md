# Role Evidence Studio: From Job Requirements to Defensible Applications

[Explore the interactive Role Evidence Studio](https://www.lauraelizabethhand.com/resources/career-professional-development-tools/reverse-ats/)

A job description sets out what an employer wants. A résumé or portfolio shows only a portion of what a candidate has done. The distance between those two documents is not always a skills gap. Sometimes the relevant experience is missing from the application; sometimes it is described without enough evidence; sometimes a qualification really does need to be developed.

I built Role Evidence Studio to make those distinctions easier to examine. The purpose is not to declare someone qualified or unqualified. It is to identify what a claim rests on, how the available evidence can be used, and what remains uncertain.

## Read a posting as a set of claims to test

The analysis workspace separates requirements into categories: essential qualifications, preferred skills, responsibilities, technical tools, organizational context, and experience or seniority. That structure matters because they do not all call for the same kind of evidence.

A degree requirement, for example, calls for a credential. Project leadership may require examples that establish decision-making authority. A request for a particular tool calls for evidence of what the person actually did with it. Dates need to describe the relevant period of work without counting concurrent jobs twice.

The workspace helps map each requirement to candidate material. The source remains attached so an assertion can be traced back to a résumé, portfolio, document, public reference, or direct account.

## Separate visibility from support

A résumé can name a technical skill without demonstrating proficiency. A project can report a substantial outcome without proving that one person independently caused it. A record can be genuine but too confidential to reproduce publicly.

Role Evidence Studio therefore distinguishes what is **visible in the supplied material** from what is **supported after review**. Users can mark the source and verification state of an evidence item, identify authority or chronology concerns, and restrict what may be shared.

That distinction changes how an application is revised. A visibility gap may justify a more precise résumé bullet. An authority gap may require confirming the scope of a role. A genuine skills gap may call for further learning or practice. The response should fit the problem, not merely make the language stronger.

## Work across a career history

Career histories can involve overlapping roles, consulting, academic appointments, volunteer work, and projects that cross institutional boundaries. The prototype supports reviewed date intervals and checks overlapping years when assessing duration requirements. It does not treat two concurrent positions as twice the passage of time.

The separate Career Discovery & Tracking workspace helps people explore possible role families, organize opportunities, and record application progress. A shared candidate profile can be transferred between the two workspaces, with controls intended to exclude evidence that should not travel with it.

## Keep the final claim within the evidence

A useful career-development tool should sometimes recommend a rewrite, and sometimes recommend more investigation. It should be able to distinguish:

- Experience that can be described more clearly.
- Experience whose dates, scope, or level of authority still require support.
- Evidence that is relevant but not suitable for public disclosure.
- Requirements for which no persuasive support has yet been provided.

In practice, that means reviewing what is true, what can be established, and what the intended reader needs to see before deciding how to present it.

## Try the demonstration

Open the [Role Evidence Studio](https://www.lauraelizabethhand.com/resources/career-professional-development-tools/reverse-ats/) and start with the fictional sample posting and candidate. Examine the requirement matrix, inspect the supporting evidence, and see how different gaps lead to different suggested actions. The interface also supports a second workspace for career discovery and opportunity tracking.

Inputs are processed in the browser by default. Some optional public-profile lookups involve network requests. Browser-local saving and JSON/CSV export are user-controlled, and exported files can contain personal information. Users should avoid entering or sharing information they are not authorized to disclose.

**Interpretation limits:** The tool offers structured guidance for human review. It does not verify credentials automatically, reproduce an employer's proprietary selection process, predict hiring outcomes, or determine someone's fitness for a job. Salary figures without a cited posting range are estimates, not verified offers.
