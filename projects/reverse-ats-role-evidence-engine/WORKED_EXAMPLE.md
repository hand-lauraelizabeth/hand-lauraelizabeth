# From a Job Requirement to a Defensible Claim

A 10-minute fictional exercise for [Role Evidence Studio](https://www.lauraelizabethhand.com/resources/career-professional-development-tools/reverse-ats/).

Use the built-in sample job and sample evidence. The people, responsibilities, and numbers in this exercise are fictional. No personal résumé or confidential document is needed.

## 1. Identify the question before changing the wording

Load the sample posting and sample candidate in **Role Evidence Analysis**, then select **Analyze**. The posting includes an eight-year experience requirement, program leadership, people development, contracts, and budget responsibilities.

The candidate describes curriculum and program work, coordinating 12 courses, training 18 facilitators, creating dashboards and QA rubrics, participating in contract reviews, and contributing to roadmap planning. The example expressly leaves direct-report relationships, budget approval, and final roadmap decision rights unestablished.

Treat each requirement as a different evidence question. A tool name, an elapsed-duration requirement, and a claim of budget authority cannot be established by the same keyword match.

## 2. Inspect the evidence behind a match

In the requirement matrix, locate the evidence-node ID attached to the experience requirement. Select that node in **Review an evidence node**.

Record a fictional source reference, such as “Synthetic exercise record A.” Enter **2017–2026** in the reviewed date field. Mark the evidence **Verified** and **Public/shareable**, then save the reviewed metadata.

Inspect the requirement again. The supporting statement can have a reviewed status while its elapsed duration remains unresolved. Ten distinct calendar years touched by a range do not establish ten elapsed years of relevant experience. Exact dates, gaps, the nature of the work, and overlapping roles still matter.

**Decision:** retain a verification action for the eight-year requirement. Do not convert a calendar-coverage count into an elapsed-experience claim.

## 3. Separate contribution from authority

Review the requirements involving people, contracts, and budgets. Compare each with the sample's wording.

| Evidence in the example | What it can help describe | What still needs support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinated 12 courses | Program coordination and delivery scope | Sole ownership of the portfolio or roadmap |
| Trained 18 facilitators | Training and facilitator development | Eighteen direct reports or supervisory authority |
| Participated in contract reviews | A contribution to contract-review work | Signing authority, procurement ownership, or budget approval |
| Contributed to roadmap planning | Planning and collaboration | Final prioritization or resource-allocation decisions |

**Decision:** use the contribution that is documented. Recover another source if the posting requires authority the current example does not establish.

## 4. Compare two possible revisions

**Defensible from the fictional record:** “Coordinated 12 courses and trained 18 facilitators across three program cycles; built evaluation dashboards and curriculum QA rubrics.”

**Not established by this record:** “Directed an 18-person team, owned program budgets, and independently set the portfolio roadmap.”

The second sentence changes the claim rather than improving the first sentence's clarity. A persuasive application needs the right evidence before it needs stronger verbs.

## 5. Produce a review record

Export the requirement matrix and record a next action for each unresolved question. If you transfer a shared profile to **Career Discovery & Tracking**, review the confidentiality labels first. The transfer excludes evidence explicitly marked private, anonymize-only, or do-not-use; unspecified labels still need review. Exported files may contain whatever text you supplied.

| Question | Next evidence to recover | Decision until then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the chronology establish the required duration? | Relevant exact start/end dates and overlap review | Duration unresolved |
| Did the candidate supervise people? | Confirmed reporting relationships and management responsibilities | Describe training or coordination only |
| Did the candidate control budgets or contracts? | Documented approval, decision, or signing authority | Describe the supported contribution |
| Can the evidence be shared? | Permission or a suitable public/anonymized alternative | Review disclosure before transfer |

## Reflection

Which finding called for a clearer description? Which called for more evidence? Which might call for further experience or a different opportunity? Explain the difference using the source, rather than a match percentage.

This exercise demonstrates a review method. It does not predict employer rankings or hiring decisions. See the [design and evidence method](ROLE_EVIDENCE_METHOD.md) and [implementation](README.md).
