Beyond the Pooled Effect

Interactive lesson · R statistics · synthetic study data

All numbers below are invented for teaching. The calculations are browser-local and illustrate fixed-effect inverse-variance weighting, Cochran’s Q and descriptive I². They do not reproduce the REML random-effects model in the accompanying R teaching script, nor establish treatment effectiveness.

Learning objective

Calculate a weighted pooled estimate, distinguish study precision from study size, and explain why a pooled result should be interpreted alongside between-study variation.

3. Applied capstone

A colleague writes: “The pooled estimate is positive, so every study supports the same conclusion.” Explain what is missing from that inference. Use the study estimates and I², and identify one additional check before interpreting real research.

Three criteria (0–2 each):
  1. Identifies the pooled estimate as an average, not universal agreement (0 absent, 1 partial, 2 explicit).
  2. Uses at least one study estimate and I² correctly (0 neither, 1 one, 2 both).
  3. Names an appropriate additional check (e.g., study quality, sensitivity, effect definition, or random-effects assumptions) without claiming causal proof (0 absent, 1 vague, 2 specific).
