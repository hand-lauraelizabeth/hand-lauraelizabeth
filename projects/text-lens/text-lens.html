Method note. Readability formulas and syllable counts are English-language heuristics. Sentence boundaries follow terminal punctuation, not physical line wraps; abbreviations and dialogue remain imperfect. The evidence register includes short source excerpts and source line/character offsets to support verification. The contextual sentiment panel is an interpretable English-language lexicon heuristic accounting for local negation, modifiers, and contrast, not a trained model or a reliable assessment of intent, irony, quotation attribution, emotion, quality, or overall meaning. Frequency, collocation, concordance, and similarity measures describe surface lexical patterns; interpretation remains with the reader. PMI can over-emphasize rare pairs, so Text Lens requires a user-selected minimum occurrence count. Cross-document distinctive terms use signed log-likelihood G² with a separate minimum-frequency floor and can compare against the remainder of the corpus, one document, or a custom reference set; lexical similarity is not semantic similarity. Saved corpus setups contain settings and file metadata/order, not source text. Affective and thematic categories use an independently curated English-language cue vocabulary. They are descriptive indicators, not validated emotion diagnoses, business outcome measures, or evidence of authorial intent. Research design references: VADER contextual rules; NRC emotion dimensions (the NRC lexicon is not included or redistributed); research on narrative emotional arcs; and cross-domain aspect sentiment research.
