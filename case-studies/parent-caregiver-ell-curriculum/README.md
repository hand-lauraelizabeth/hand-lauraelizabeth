# Parent & Caregiver ELL Curriculum — Project Light + CRD Impact (2020)

## Case study summary

In 2020, Laura Elizabeth Hand worked with CRD Impact owner Cassandra Decker and Project Light on a parent/caregiver English-language-learning curriculum designed around the practical demands adult learners faced while navigating schools, healthcare, community resources, and digital systems.

The project extended well beyond a conventional lesson-plan assignment. The work included:

- a ten-module ELL/platform curriculum with supplementary materials;
- learner-facing materials;
- teacher/facilitator materials;
- administrator/progress guidance;
- intake and assessment planning;
- digital-literacy instruction;
- mixed-level scaffolding;
- synchronous, virtual, asynchronous, and disrupted-delivery planning;
- grant, reporting, and compliance requirements;
- COVID-era access and delivery adaptations.

This project is separate from Laura Elizabeth Hand's Community Impact ESOL work.

## Original project scope and timeline

An August 23, 2020 project plan states:

- **Project:** create ten ELL/platform modules with supplementary materials in collaboration with Cassandra Decker.
- **First five modules:** targeted for completion by September 14, 2020.
- **Remaining five modules:** targeted before October 2, 2020.
- **Primary initial mode:** synchronous, on-site ELL sessions.
- **Required layers:** teacher materials, student materials, and administrator progress guides.
- **Planned extensions:** virtual/remote delivery, asynchronous learning, more advanced ELL materials, basic-literacy support, and possible child/family-learning extensions.

The same plan records rapid iterative drafting across August and September: initial structural work, lesson-one drafting and revision, first-week/module-one completion, review with Cassandra Decker, then rolling development and revision of modules two through five.

## Design constraints

### Needs-assessment and grant requirements

The curriculum was not designed in a vacuum. The project plan ties initial learner level and program structure to stakeholder feedback and needs assessments underlying grant funding.

The documents reference requirements connected to multiple funding/program streams, including research, implementation, tracking, and COVID adaptation. For portfolio purposes, this is important because it shows curriculum design operating within programmatic, reporting, and implementation constraints—not only pedagogical preferences.

### Mixed learner levels

The initial design centered primarily on beginning adult ELL levels while anticipating learners above that range. The teacher materials therefore include scaffolds, extensions, flexible grouping, comprehension checks, and embedded language development rather than assuming a homogeneous cohort.

### COVID-era delivery uncertainty

The 2020 context required the curriculum to support shifting delivery conditions. The planning documents anticipate:

- in-person classes with public-health constraints;
- synchronous virtual delivery;
- asynchronous/self-led learning;
- access to phones, tablets, laptops, internet, and projected content;
- different participation schedules;
- learner support outside a conventional classroom.

## Three connected users

A distinctive strength of the project was that the curriculum was designed simultaneously for three user groups.

### Learners

Student-facing materials supported practical language, digital, and navigation skills. Topics included:

- introductions and self-advocacy;
- school systems and educational resources;
- school and medical navigation;
- food, shopping, science, health, and math contexts;
- movement and mental health;
- reading strategies;
- college/education navigation;
- digital literacy.

### Teachers / facilitators

Teacher materials included more than activity directions. They also preserved:

- rationale;
- objectives;
- materials;
- activity sequencing;
- comprehension checks;
- scaffolding suggestions;
- mixed-level adaptation;
- embedded language/grammar opportunities;
- observations to make during delivery;
- alternative approaches;
- links to supporting resources.

### Administrators / program operations

The project plan explicitly calls for administrator progress guides. The materials also connect enrollment, assessment, learner goals, digital access, progress tracking, compliance documentation, and program reporting.

That architecture treats curriculum as both a learning system and an operating system.

## Designing around real-world use

The materials repeatedly start from what adult learners needed to **do**, not simply what language content an instructor needed to cover.

Examples include:

- complete and interpret forms;
- create/use email;
- navigate a browser or digital form;
- locate information using headings, tables of contents, maps, and links;
- ask for clarification;
- make appointments;
- communicate with schools;
- interpret school resources;
- identify community services;
- set goals;
- support children's educational participation;
- advocate for themselves and their families.

Language, literacy, digital fluency, and institutional navigation were therefore integrated rather than treated as unrelated subjects.

## Accessibility and learner support

The materials show attention to access through:

- first-language support where useful;
- multimodal directions;
- explicit modeling;
- scaffolded practice;
- repeated comprehension checks;
- technology support;
- mixed-level adaptation;
- reduced assumptions about prior familiarity with U.S. educational systems;
- alternative delivery modes.

Program planning also addresses ADA/access considerations alongside instructional and technology access.

## Assessment and evidence

The program materials connect learning to observable performance and progress evidence, including:

- intake and baseline assessment;
- participation and comprehension checks;
- practical task completion;
- learner goals;
- language/digital skill demonstrations;
- progress documentation;
- compliance/reporting records.

This made assessment part of the curriculum workflow rather than a separate end-stage activity.

## What this case demonstrates

- rapid curriculum architecture under real deadlines;
- multi-layer design for learners, instructors, and administrators;
- needs-assessment-informed curriculum development;
- adult learning and ESOL pedagogy;
- digital-literacy integration;
- school/community-system navigation;
- mixed-level scaffolding;
- accessibility-aware design;
- synchronous/asynchronous planning;
- facilitator enablement;
- assessment and compliance integration;
- iterative revision with a program partner.
